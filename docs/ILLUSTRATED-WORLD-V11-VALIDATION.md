## Ground material and shoreline findings

The existing Native land uses64-unit categorical cells, an abrupt tan threshold below12m and a flat per-cell elevation tint. Simply tinting those same rectangles cannot remove the grid. The present shoreline sprites preserve the categorical mask and inherit its straight/right-angle geometry. Composition2 does not alter either cause.

Recommended **next proof, not implemented**: reuse the256 Native terrain slots as ImageLabels carrying world-aligned crops of one quiet periodic ground material. Each64-unit rectangle retains exactly the original land/water ownership; keep Native background colours beneath loading/failed textures. Adjacent land cells must share crop phase and continuous field-derived colour at their boundary. A continuous texture beneath independent hard tints would still show seams. Evaluate a single pre-baked continuous colour/material study first; do not add a gradient object per tile or a whole-country static background. Filtering, palette, coast joins, crop seams and loading fallback require a later approved hosted-image test.

Shoreline conclusions:

- Palette-matched material and a land-side painted overlap can soften the **interior** join without changing ownership. Current tan/green mismatch remains especially visible at the diagonal close-up.
- Existing shore rocks and irregular painted edges provide fine visual detail, but cannot turn the categorical boundary into a genuinely curved coast.
- Adding vegetation to hide every corner would be misleading and expensive; composition keeps whole footprints inland instead.
- Curving land/water coverage beyond the existing ownership mask is a **separate visual geometry proposal**: it would need a defined authoritative-vs-visual boundary tolerance, corner/diagonal connectivity rules, sector-edge contract, construction overlay clarity and revised coverage tests. Not implemented or approved here.
